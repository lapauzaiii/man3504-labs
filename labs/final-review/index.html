What you should be able to do

Open each week. If you can do everything listed, you are ready for that part of the exam.

Weeks 1–6: Foundations through operations planning 12 questions · 30 points
  • Week 1. Name the competitive priorities (cost, quality, time, flexibility) and tell an order qualifier from an order winner.
  • Week 2. Classify a process (job, batch, line, continuous) and find the bottleneck and maximum output of a simple line.
  • Week 3. Distinguish design and effective capacity; compute utilization and efficiency; explain capacity cushion; apply the Theory of Constraints steps in order.
  • Week 4. Compute moving average and exponential smoothing forecasts; compute and interpret MAD and the tracking signal.
  • Week 5. Use a regression equation to forecast; choose an appropriate qualitative method (e.g., Delphi) when there is no history.
  • Week 6. Compare chase and level aggregate plans; compute an MRP net requirement; explain how the aggregate plan, MPS and MRP connect.
Utilization = actual ÷ design capacity  ·  Efficiency = actual ÷ effective capacity
Fₜ₊₁ = Fₜ + α(Aₜ − Fₜ)  ·  Tracking signal = CFE ÷ MAD (review beyond ±4)
Net requirement = gross requirement − projected on-hand − scheduled receipts
Week 8: Supply chain management 6 questions · 15 points
  • Match efficient and responsive supply chain designs to product demand characteristics.
  • Define the bullwhip effect and identify practices that amplify or dampen it.
  • Compute weeks of supply and inventory turnover.
  • Find the make-or-buy break-even quantity and explain what else matters besides cost.
Weeks of supply = average aggregate inventory value ÷ weekly cost of goods sold
Inventory turnover = annual cost of goods sold ÷ average aggregate inventory value
Make-or-buy break-even Q = (F_make − F_buy) ÷ (c_buy − c_make)
Week 9: Inventory management 8 questions · 20 points
  • Compute EOQ and the total annual ordering and holding cost.
  • Compute safety stock and the reorder point.
  • Compare continuous (Q) and periodic (P) review systems.
  • Apply ABC classification by dollar usage.
  • Recognize when EOQ's assumptions fail (perishability, service capacity that cannot be stored).
EOQ = √(2DS ÷ H)  ·  Annual cost = (D ÷ Q)S + (Q ÷ 2)H
Safety stock = z × σ_d × √L  ·  ROP = d × L + safety stock
Week 10: Lean systems and vendor relationships 7 questions · 17.5 points
  • Compute takt time and compare it with step cycle times.
  • Compute process cycle efficiency and identify where to cut non-value-added time safely.
  • Compute the number of kanban containers (always round up).
  • Classify the seven wastes; explain push versus pull.
  • Describe lean supplier relationships.
Takt time = available time ÷ demand  ·  PCE = value-added time ÷ total lead time
Kanban containers k = d(w + p)(1 + α) ÷ c, rounded up
Week 11: Quality and performance management 7 questions · 17.5 points
  • Classify costs as prevention, appraisal, internal failure, or external failure, and compute shares of total cost of quality.
  • Compute DPMO.
  • Compute Cp and Cpk, and decide whether to recenter or reduce variation.
  • Explain Deming's view of system-caused problems, TQM, and the DMAIC phases.
DPMO = defects ÷ (units × opportunities) × 1,000,000
Cp = (USL − LSL) ÷ 6σ  ·  Cpk = min[(x̄ − LSL) ÷ 3σ, (USL − x̄) ÷ 3σ]
Week 12: Statistical process control 8 questions · 20 points
  • Compute X-bar and R chart limits from factor tables (factors will be given).
  • Compute p-chart and c-chart limits (an LCL below zero is set to 0).
  • Choose the right chart for the data.
  • Apply run rules; distinguish common from special causes; explain tampering.
  • Distinguish control limits from specification limits, and stability from capability.
X-bar: X̄̄ ± A₂R̄  ·  R: UCL = D₄R̄, LCL = D₃R̄
p: p̄ ± 3√(p̄(1 − p̄) ÷ n)  ·  c: c̄ ± 3√c̄
Week 13: Ethics, sustainability, and global operations 6 questions · 15 points
  • Build a total cost of ownership comparison (price, freight, defects, safety stock, expected disruption) and explain why invoice price misleads.
  • Compute revenue at risk from a sourcing claim.
  • Classify measures by Triple Bottom Line dimension.
  • Distinguish offshoring, nearshoring, and reshoring.
  • Interpret an audit refusal; explain the 100% Rule and scope creep in a WBS.
Defect cost per unit = defect rate × price  ·  Expected disruption cost per unit = (disruptions per year × cost each) ÷ annual volume
Revenue at risk = share tied to premium × total revenue × premium
Week 14: Performance measurement and continuous improvement 6 questions · 15 points
  • Place metrics in Balanced Scorecard perspectives and read lagging versus leading patterns.
  • Identify competitive, internal, functional, and reverse benchmarking.
  • Place activities in PDCA phases, including both outcomes of Act.
  • Explain Deming's funnel rules and overcontrol.
  • Compute a PERT expected time and variance, and the probability of meeting a deadline.
te = (a + 4m + b) ÷ 6  ·  σ² = ((b − a) ÷ 6)²
z = (target − expected length) ÷ √(sum of critical-path variances)
